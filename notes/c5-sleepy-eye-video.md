# C5 Corvette sleepy-eye LED headlight video: notes

Source: "C5 Corvette Sleepy Eye TripLED Headlight Install How-To Official" by KnightDriveTV
(https://youtu.be/5p7CBdkqv_8), 1:22:12 long, recorded February 2023. It covers kits
shipped since December 2022.

## How these notes were made

- **Audio:** the full soundtrack, transcribed with speech-to-text, came from the copy
  shared on Google Drive. Every measurement and instruction below comes from what he
  says. Timestamps are in brackets.
- **Pictures:** a second copy of the video (720p, with picture) was shared later. Still
  frames were pulled from the sections that matter; see "How the lights attach" below.
- The transcript itself is not in this repo. It is the creator's content; these are
  summary notes.

## The kit

- Each side is a row of three LED projectors, called "the triplets". The kit comes in
  square and round lens styles, and the video covers both [0:00:14].
- **Which unit goes where:** the unit with the high beam at the front is the driver
  side [0:07:10]. Each side has two outer low beams and one inner high beam, the one
  toward the hood [1:09:01].
- **Each projector has a halo (ring light):**
  - Parking lights: all six halos on.
  - Low beam: the four outer lows on, all halos on.
  - High beam, stock wiring: only the two inner highs on, halos on.
  - High beam with the optional relay: all six on [0:07:48, 1:08:54].
- **In the box:**
  - Two main metal stops. They are side-specific: the driver's has a closed hole at the
    top, an angled middle and an open slot at the bottom [0:26:49].
  - Two black stabilizing stops.
  - The all-six-on-high relay, one T-tap (a clip-on wire tap) per side, and an optional
    resistor.
  - A hardware bag: six 25 mm and two 30 mm M6×1 bolts, nuts, lock washers and flat
    washers [0:27:51, 0:33:35].
- **Install time:** about 2 hours for a shop, 4–6 hours at home [0:01:09].

## How the sleepy-eye height is set (the key numbers for a shroud)

- **The headlight doors aren't modified.** A metal stop bolts next to the headlight
  motor. A bolt on the stop blocks the headlight arm, so the door stops partway up.
- **Target height: 5¼ in (about 133 mm).** Measure from the top of the frame rail to
  the **leading (front) edge** of the headlight top plate [0:36:09–0:38:14].
  - He says the old target was 5½ in, and the right value is somewhere from 5¼ to
    5½ in (about 133–140 mm). He picked 5¼ in with the bezels they were developing in
    mind [0:36:44–0:37:25].
  - Measuring at the back of the plate reads lower than at the front, so the leading
    edge is the reference.
- **How it's set:** raise the door by hand with the knob on the motor until the
  front edge reads 5¼ in. Screw the stop bolt up until it touches the underside of the
  arm, then lock it with the jam nut [0:38:22–0:40:10].
  - His came out about 1/16 in high. He suggests setting 1/16 in low, since tightening
    the jam nut raises it slightly [0:40:56–0:41:21].
- **Stop hardware:**
  - The stack on the bolt is nut, then lock washer, then flat washer. The flat washer
    takes the load against the tab, because the nut on the stop is only tack-welded
    [0:28:57–0:30:16].
  - **Early cars (1997 to about mid-2000):** the motor housing is all metal with a
    round motor. Remove the lower motor bolt and use the 30 mm bolt [0:21:23, 0:27:51].
  - **Late cars (about mid-2000 to 2004):** the housing is plastic with a rectangular
    motor and three motor bolts in a triangle. Remove the white stopper from the tab,
    fit the stop's lower slot over that tab, and use a 25 mm bolt [0:22:46–0:23:19,
    0:33:22–0:34:51].
- **Stabilizing stops:**
  - The two black stops replace the white rubber bumpers at the back, with the
    letter "O" on top and facing the windshield [1:04:01–1:05:28].
  - They should only lightly touch. The metal stop carries the load; the black stops
    just keep the lights from fluttering [1:05:41, 1:06:19].

## How the lights attach (seen on video)

- **What the kit is:** each side is a complete replacement for the stock headlight
  unit. It isn't bulbs that go into the stock housing. Three black cube-shaped projector
  housings, each with "KNIGHTDRIVETV" on top, sit side by side on one black steel
  bracket. The wiring harness and plugs hang off the back [0:07:14–0:07:56].
- **It goes exactly where the stock unit was.** He unbolts the whole stock
  unit and hands it out of the car [0:17:25–0:18:35]. The kit bracket bolts to the same
  three points, using the car's own adjusters [0:58:20–1:03:06].
  - **Outer (fender) side, two bolts.** The kit bracket has a flat black tab with holes.
    He pushes each bolt through from behind, holds it with his thumb, and adds a lock
    washer and nut on the front [0:59:11–1:00:36]. The long threaded rod of the
    up/down adjuster runs just above this point.
  - **Inner (hood) side, one bolt.** This is the car's silver cast left/right adjuster
    pad, which has two holes. A stud on the kit goes through the **upper** hole, then a
    lock washer and nut [1:01:39–1:02:15].
  - **Top: the 10 mm nut** on the up/down adjuster goes back on last [1:02:54].
- **Once installed,** the three cubes sit in the headlight well directly under the
  headlight door's frame. The cube tops are close to the door's underside
  [1:02:32, 1:07:32].
- **From the front,** with the stock bezel off and the door in the sleepy position,
  there's an open rounded-rectangle hole in the bodywork. The three lit cubes show at the
  bottom of it, and the door's gray frame and linkage are visible above them
  [0:53:16]. That exposed space around and above the cubes is the space a shroud would
  fill.
- **The stock bezel** is the black plastic surround around the stock headlight, just
  under the door's front edge. It unclips by pulling it forward and rotating it down
  [0:13:01–0:13:13].
- **The metal stops** are two flat steel plates. Each has a closed slot at the top and
  an open slot at the bottom, with a bolt and nut already fitted. The two black rubber
  stabilizing stops are small squares [0:07:28–0:07:35].

## Mounting the light assembly

- The outer (fender) side mounts with two bolts, the inner (hood) side with one bolt
  [0:41:40].
- On the older cars, the bottom position on the two-bolt side takes a 25 mm bolt,
  since the 30 mm one went into the stop. On the later cars that bottom position takes
  a 30 mm bolt [0:58:59–0:59:30].
- **The inner mounting pad has two holes. Use the upper one.** The lower hole tips the
  lights downward [1:01:39–1:01:56].
- The up/down and left/right adjusters often haven't moved since the factory and may
  be seized. Soak them in penetrating oil, or buy replacements [0:20:03–0:21:00].
  - He adjusts them with an E8 socket wrench; a ¼ in socket also works [1:19:07–1:20:04].
- **At the end, the top covers (4 × T15 screws) go back on** [1:07:52, 1:18:56].
  - **The stock headlight bezel, removed in step 3, is never refitted.** He mentions
    that KnightDriveTV was working on its own bezels [0:36:56]. The space where the
    bezel was is the space a shroud fills.

## How the stock bezel attaches (research)

The stock C5 bezel is GM 10435411 (left) and 10435412 (right). It's a black plastic
surround.
- **Front:** a frame round the lamp that is taller at the hood end than at the fender end.
- **Lip:** a rolled lip along the bottom.
- **Top:** a flat top with a forked tab behind it.
- **Ears:** an ear at each end. The hood-end ear is the tall one, with a round hole for
  the aiming adjuster's access plug (GM 10435410).

It's held three ways:
- **Three Phillips screws.** One goes through each ear into the side of the headlight;
  the third is at the back on the hood side.
- **The forked tab on its top,** which "slides onto a clip on the underside of the
  headlight cover". Source: Corvette Central tech blog, "C5 Headlight Repair with a T",
  steps 3 and 4 (tech.corvettecentral.com, 2011). Its step 4 photo shows the fork
  clearly.
- **Its top edge sitting in under the front of the cover.** In the KnightDriveTV video
  it comes off by "rotating downward and forward, while spreading the outer ears"
  [0:13:02–0:13:20]. At [0:13:22] the clip is visible hanging under the middle of the
  cover, just behind its front edge.

KnightDriveTV's TripLED bezel keeps this outside shape, with the top blade, fork and ears,
and adds a tunnel for each light. Their bezel is 3D-scanned from the car, and KDTV says
the Gen 2 brackets "align with the bezels".

A head-on frame at [0:13:00] gives these rough sizes, scaled to the 11 in opening:
- The bezel front is about 148 mm tall at the hood end and 102 mm at the fender end.
- The lens is about 211 × 88 mm.
- The cover's front edge slopes down toward the fender relative to the lens.

These are estimates until measured on a real bezel.

## Wiring (and what it means for clearance)

- **Halo power:**
  - Tap 12 V from a parking-light wire. His car's was the brown wire on the two-wire
    plug; the parking bulb twists out for easier reach [0:44:50–0:46:12].
  - Don't tap a headlight wire, or the halos follow the low beams.
- **Headlight plugs:**
  - The kit plugs into the stock high- and low-beam plugs. On his 1997 car the low was
    tan and the high green; the kit's low-beam plug is the one with a Y splitter
    [0:18:36, 0:46:24].
  - Polarity varies by year. If the halos are dim or uneven, flip the plug over. On his
    car both sides needed the plugs locking tab down, so they don't latch. He suggests
    tape or a zip tie to keep them together [0:48:04–0:52:49].
- **Optional resistor (only one is needed):**
  - It sits in the low-beam line. It helps the stock headlight module notice the lights
    going off and retract them [0:54:13–0:56:05].
  - Without it, the lights can stay up and powered. The fix is to go to high beam, then
    off [0:56:05–0:56:34].
  - **It gets very hot.** It's a finned, orange-anodized aluminum case. It must not
    touch anything; he zip-ties it to the headlight wiring so it hangs in open air
    near the metal frame [0:56:38–0:58:02].
- **Relay (all six on high):**
  - Disconnect the battery first. The relay's ground goes on the firewall ground stud,
    which needs sanding for a good connection. Power connects at the fuse box
    [1:09:42–1:14:36].
  - The fuse taps go on fuse 9 (high beam, right) and fuse 10 (low beam, left): black
    wire to 9, red wire to 10 [1:15:04–1:17:10].
- **Heat at the lens:** peel back the plastic lens protectors before powering the
  lights. He says the LEDs give off heat from the centers [0:49:09–0:49:46].

## What this means for the shroud design

1. **Design to a 5¼ in door height, with room up to 5½ in.** Measure from the
   frame-rail top to the front edge of the top plate. Get an owner's real measurement
   before finalizing, since cars vary.
2. **It fills the gap the stock bezel leaves.** The bezel comes off with Phillips
   screws and a clip, and isn't refitted [0:12:36–0:13:17]. Mounting to the original
   bezel screw points is the natural first choice.
3. **It frames three lenses in a row,** square or round depending on the kit. Two
   versions may be needed, or a frame that fits both.
4. **It must clear the moving headlight door and arm** through the full up/down
   cycle, in the partly open position and the fully raised one used during setup.
5. **Stay well away from the resistor** if one is fitted. For a 3D-printed part, use a
   heat-resistant material such as ASA, ABS, PETG or nylon, not PLA, near the lenses.
6. **Leave room for the plug tape or zip ties and the halo T-tap** behind the assembly.
7. **Early (1997–2000) and late (2000–2004) cars** differ only at the motor and stop.
   The front opening should be the same on both, so one shroud probably fits both; this
   isn't confirmed.
